# Narrative revision

Read this reference for fiction, scripts, personal essays, case studies, or other prose
whose effect depends on character, chronology, revelation, scene order, or an ending.

## Research basis and limit

This guidance adapts the findings of *StoryScope: Investigating Idiosyncrasies in AI
Fiction* (Russell et al., COLM 2026, arXiv:2604.03136v6). The paper compares 10,272 human
stories with parallel stories from five language models. Narrative features alone separate
human and AI stories at 93.2% macro-F1. After surface-level AI artifacts were edited out,
the narrative classifier still reached 93.9% macro-F1. The durable differences were choices
about theme, causality, resolution, time, revelation, setting, character, and reader
relationship.

These are corpus-level tendencies, not laws of good writing and not proof about an
individual text. They were measured on long-form fiction. Use them as diagnostic prompts;
do not mechanically invert every AI-associated feature or claim that they transfer with
the same strength to other genres.

## Map the current narrative

Before changing sentences, make a compact private map:

- **Agents:** who drives events, how characters enter, what they want, and how they change;
- **Social network:** which relationships endure, collide, or remain peripheral;
- **Events and causality:** the main beats, causal links, accidents, omissions, and loose ends;
- **Plot and theme:** conflict, subplots, moral pressure, resolution, and what the narrator
  explains explicitly;
- **Setting:** locations, spatial detail, atmosphere, and whether the environment mirrors
  emotion too neatly;
- **Time:** order, duration, compression, repetition, flashbacks, flash-forwards, and gaps;
- **Revelation:** what is withheld, when it arrives, and whether it changes the meaning of
  earlier scenes;
- **Perspective:** narrator, focal characters, distance, dialogue, and address to the reader;
- **Prose texture:** rhythm, diction, imagery, allusion, and sensory density.

Diagnose the dominant two to four defaults. Rebuild those choices before line editing.
Trying to change every dimension usually produces a conspicuously engineered story.

## Structural defaults to challenge

| Default found in AI stories | Revision question | Useful intervention when earned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The theme or moral is stated repeatedly | Has the action already made the meaning available? | Remove narrator commentary, let a choice carry the theme, or leave two interpretations alive. |
| Dialogue becomes a philosophical debate | Would this character really say the thesis aloud? | Give the speaker a local motive, let them evade the point, or move meaning into action and subtext. |
| Every flourish and subplot serves one theme | Does the story feel over-designed? | Keep a detail with another function; add or strengthen a parallel, contrasting, or independent thread. |
| One continuous causal chain drives everything | Is each beat merely the clean consequence of the previous beat? | Permit coincidence, delayed consequence, incomplete knowledge, competing causes, or a consequential omission. |
| The protagonist's choice neatly resolves the conflict | Is agency being rewarded too predictably? | Let the choice fail partly, arrive too late, solve one problem while creating another, or meet forces outside the character's control. |
| Resolution arrives as internal understanding or acceptance | Does insight substitute for consequence? | Resolve through action, relationship, external change, partial failure, or leave the central pressure unresolved. |
| There are no meaningful subplots or loose ends | Has everything been streamlined into a single track? | Restore a secondary pressure that changes how the main action reads. Do not add filler. |
| The chronology is linear and revelation arrives on schedule | Could order create productive uncertainty? | Delay context, use a time jump, revisit an event, or reveal information that recontextualizes an earlier scene. |
| The protagonist is morally legible | Does the narration tell the reader how to judge the choice? | Preserve mixed motives, competing harms, biased testimony, or consequences that resist a clean verdict. |
| Emotion is repeatedly embodied | Does every feeling become a tight throat, cold skin, or clenched hand? | Use behavior, speech, omission, an explicit emotion label, or no explanation. Select one telling physical detail at most. |
| Setting mirrors the character's mind | Does weather, light, and landscape perform the emotion for the scene? | Let the setting be indifferent, practically inconvenient, socially meaningful, or emotionally counterpointed. |
| Sensory description is dense across the whole story | Is description proving that the prose is vivid? | Keep only details that orient, alter action, reveal attention, or become consequential. Uneven density is fine. |
| A character enters through external description | Does the story pause to render a portrait? | Introduce the character in action, dialogue, thought, or another person's partial report. |
| References remain vague and generic | Would this person or world know something more specific? | Use a precise, verified work, place, object, brand, ritual, or cultural reference when it belongs there. Never fabricate one. |
| The narration behaves as if no reader exists | Would the chosen voice acknowledge an audience? | Consider an aside, withheld explanation, direct address, self-correction, or fourth-wall pressure when the genre supports it. |
| The ending explains what changed | Does the final paragraph translate the story for the reader? | End on the consequence, image, decision, contradiction, or unanswered pressure. Cut the lesson after it. |

Do not force flashbacks, direct reader address, named references, subplots, or ambiguity into
a story that does not need them. A simple linear story can be distinctly human when its
specific choices arise from character and context.

## Create coherent rarity

In the StoryScope corpus, human stories occupied a broader and rarer region of narrative
feature space: mean rarity percentile 0.71 for humans versus 0.49 for AI. Rarity here means
an unusual combination of narrative decisions, not random novelty.

Give the piece at least one choice that is both specific and consequential:

- a character notices the detail their history or job would make salient;
- a social obligation competes with the obvious plot goal;
- a setting constrains action in a concrete way;
- a revelation changes the interpretation of an earlier beat instead of merely adding
  information;
- the ending resolves the event but complicates the relationship, or the reverse;
- chronology follows memory, evidence, ritual, or consequence rather than a generic arc.

The choice should propagate through the text. If it can be removed without changing
anything else, it is decoration rather than authorship.

## Rebuild in two passes

### 1. Narrative pass

Revise the private map. Move, remove, add, or alter beats only as needed to address the
dominant defaults. Check that character motives, causal links, knowledge states, and time
references remain coherent after the change.

Do not add factual material to personal essays or case studies. Structural humanization in
nonfiction must come from the supplied events and evidence.

### 2. Prose pass

Rewrite the language after the structure works:

- cut thematic commentary that the scene already earns;
- remove generic bodily metaphors and decorative sensory stacks;
- let dialogue pursue character goals rather than explain the story;
- vary scene length and narrative distance according to pressure;
- preserve purposeful repetition, silence, abruptness, and plain emotion labels;
- keep specific details; remove details that merely advertise vivid writing;
- make transitions clear enough without narrating every connection.

## Narrative review

Ask privately:

- What does the narrator trust the reader to infer?
- Which event could be interpreted in more than one defensible way?
- Does a late fact reframe anything earlier?
- Does every consequence arrive too cleanly from a protagonist's decision?
- Are secondary characters agents or delivery systems for plot and theme?
- Do dialogue, description, and interiority have local purposes beyond atmosphere?
- Is emotion rendered through the same technique every time?
- Could the ending lose its final explanatory sentence?
- What specific combination of choices belongs to this story alone?

Fix the strongest remaining default, then stop. Do not optimize the narrative into a new
formula for looking human.
